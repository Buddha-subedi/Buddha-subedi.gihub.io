About Me

Research scientist in training, working at the intersection of AI and Earth science.

I am a PhD researcher in Water Resources Science and Engineering at the University of Minnesota, where I work on artificial intelligence, satellite remote sensing, and data-driven modeling of the Earth's atmosphere. My research focuses on developing scalable machine learning systems that transform sparse, noisy, and heterogeneous satellite observations into physically meaningful environmental information.

My technical interests span generative AI, diffusion and flow-based models, graph neural networks, transformer architectures, spatiotemporal learning, probabilistic modeling, and scientific foundation models. I am particularly interested in architectures that combine physical knowledge with data-driven learning to solve complex problems across large spatial and temporal scales.

I develop end-to-end research pipelines involving large-scale geospatial datasets, multi-sensor data integration, GPU-accelerated training, distributed computing, model evaluation, and reproducible scientific software. My work includes global precipitation generation, satellite rainfall and snowfall retrieval, stochastic super-resolution, missing-data reconstruction, and short-range precipitation forecasting.

Beyond developing predictive models, I am interested in the broader challenge of building reliable and computationally efficient AI systems that generalize across sensors, geographic regions, resolutions, and environmental conditions.

My long-term goal is to advance AI for scientific discovery by developing scalable generative and spatiotemporal learning systems that connect observations, physical processes, and predictive intelligence.

I am interested in research scientist and applied scientist opportunities in generative AI, scientific machine learning, geospatial foundation models, weather and climate intelligence, and high-performance AI computing.

Packages

TLP-R2S

Transfer Learning for Passive Microwave Rainfall and Snowfall Retrieval

TLP-R2S is a research-oriented Python package for retrieving precipitation information from passive microwave satellite observations. It uses an incremental learning framework that links atmospheric reanalysis with radar-based precipitation references.

The framework is designed to support satellite precipitation retrieval research, sensor-specific inference, and evaluation against independent precipitation datasets.
